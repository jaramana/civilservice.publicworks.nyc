About

Why

The City publishes exam schedules, civil service lists and title salaries separately. This site brings them together so readers can check application periods and published pay in one place.

Scope

The site shows published exam dates, active lists and title salaries. Open, coming-soon and closed labels are calculated from application dates. No numbers are estimated. Missing fields are left out. Confirm a date on NYC.gov before relying on it.

Built

Python, pandas and requests prepare the data and calendar files. The website uses static HTML, CSS and JavaScript, served from GitHub Pages.

Independence

No agency reviewed this site.

Credits

Data from NYC Open Data and the Department of Citywide Administrative Services (DCAS) exam pages. Median pay context comes from The Pay Gap. Claude was used in development.

Reuse

Code is BSD 3-Clause licensed. Generated data and calendar files can be reused with attribution; NYC Open Data and DCAS source material retain their own terms.

Data limits

Whether you will be hired. No published data shows how far down a list an agency has reached, or where you personally sit on it. This site has no name search or list number lookup.

How fast a list moves. The certification data shows that a list was used. It does not show how far down the list an agency went. Some titles get certified hundreds of times, because each job posting pulls just a few names. Uniformed titles get certified only a few times, in large batches instead. A high count means a title is hired often. It does not mean the list moves fast.

What you would earn. A title's salary range is what the City is allowed to pay, across every level of that job. Where we show a median instead, that is a different number from a different source. It describes what people currently in the job actually earn.

Anything about a person. The City's own data includes candidate names, exact scores, and credit flags. This site does not request those columns, store them or publish them.

Known data gaps

A missing year. No exam here has an application period between July 2024 and June 2025. The City never published that year's schedule as open data. The archive begins after that gap rather than presenting a partial year.

Open data can lag the City's own pages. When DCAS extends an application period, its web pages update first. The open data can take weeks to catch up. On one check, the City's pages listed eight open exams, but the open data listed only two. So this site reads both sources every day, and lets the City's own pages win when the two disagree.

Cut-off names. The City's title catalog cuts every name at 30 characters. About half of all titles hit that limit. Where an exam or a list spells a title out in full, we show that fuller spelling instead.

Reused exam numbers. The City reuses exam numbers about every ten years. So any history shown for an exam only covers the years after that specific list started. This keeps an old, unrelated exam's history from being credited to a new one.

Sources

Updates

A scheduled job runs the pipeline once a day. It rebuilds the files this site reads, and it commits only when something actually changed.

Exam dates are also published as a calendar. Each exam page has a button to add that exam to your own calendar, with a reminder before it closes. If you want every exam at once, the whole feed is here.

Each page gives two dates. “Current as of” is the City's schedule date; “checked” is when this site last read its sources. A recent check can still contain an older City schedule.
